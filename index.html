One company is 17 times bigger. The smaller one pays its owners more.

Square Pharmaceuticals earns 27% more on every taka of assets than Ibn Sina does. Ibn Sina still returns more to shareholders — 19.0% against 14.7% — because it runs its balance sheet twice as hard and borrows to do it. What each balance sheet is made of

Square's assets are mostly cash — 52.3bn of its 69.7bn current assets, or 75%. That idle cash is the single biggest reason its asset turnover looks weak next to a company a fraction of its size.

Return on equity, decomposed

Where the returns actually come from

DuPont splits ROE into three things a manager can act on: margin, how hard assets work, and how much of the balance sheet is borrowed. Multiply the three and the result must land exactly on ROE. That identity is what exposed the error in the 2023 row.
